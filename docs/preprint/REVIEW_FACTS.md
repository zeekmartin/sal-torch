### A1.1 The BERT comparison is not budget-matched

| arm | training epochs | recovery epochs | total passes over the training split | pruned when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DropHead | 3 (with head masking) | **0** | **3** | during training, resampled every step; evaluated pruned |
| magnitude + recovery | 3 (dense) | 2 | **5** | after training, then recovered |

**The magnitude arm sees 5 passes over the training data and the DropHead arm sees 3.** The paper reports magnitude beating DropHead on 8 of 8 cells by 0.42 to 7.03 points; part of that margin is 2 extra epochs, and the experiment as run cannot separate the two. Nothing in the result files contains a DropHead-plus-recovery arm, so the separation cannot be recovered from what exists -- it needs a run.

Two cheap arms would settle it, and both are in scope for the 10-seed campaign: **DropHead + 2 epochs of recovery** (equalises total budget at 5 and is the arm a practitioner would actually deploy), and **magnitude + 0 epochs**, which the files already have as `magnitude_oneshot_accuracy` (66.03% at MNLI P50 against DropHead's 79.54%, i.e. DropHead wins by 13.51pp when neither arm recovers).

**At zero recovery for both arms, the ordering reverses on every cell.** That is the comparison the paper's resilience claim rests on, and it is in the committed data:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mnli | 33% | 82.31% | 79.45% | **+2.86pp** | 83.92% | magnitude |
| mnli | 50% | 79.58% | 66.92% | **+12.66pp** | 83.35% | magnitude |
| mrpc | 33% | 75.82% | 75.49% | **+0.33pp** | 82.84% | magnitude |

### A1.2 exp24 is matched on recovery, not on total training

Every exp24 arm gets the same 5 recovery epochs on the same data in the same order, which is what makes the comparison legitimate: it asks whether the masked weights are a better starting point. But the masked arms also had a full training run of their own beforehand and the pretrained arm did not, so the total compute differs by that run. This matters for the cost argument, not for the accuracy conclusion: it means the masked arms are *more* expensive and still do not win, which strengthens rather than weakens the finding.

## A2. Latency: protocol and dispersion

### A2.1 The protocol, as implemented

`exp26_mlp_posthoc.eager_latency`, imported unchanged by exp39, exp41, exp43 and exp46:

| element | what the code does |
|---|---|
| input | `torch.randn(batch, 3, 224, 224)` on device: synthetic, not real images; weights and inputs do not affect timing |
| warm-up | 20 forwards, then ONE `torch.cuda.synchronize()` |
| timed loop | 100 iterations; per iteration `perf_counter` -> forward -> **`torch.cuda.synchronize()`** -> record. The sync is inside the loop, so each sample is a complete forward |
| statistic | median of the 100 samples |
| p10 / p90 | `sorted(t)[n//10]` and `sorted(t)[-max(1, n//10)]`, i.e. the 11th smallest and 11th largest of 100: order statistics, not interpolated quantiles |
| throughput | `batch / (median_ms / 1000)` |
| mode | eager; no `torch.compile`, no CUDA graphs |
| memory | **not measured** -- see A2.3 |

Why the median: "a single MIOpen recompilation or a neighbouring job on the node moves the mean and not the median" (`exp26_mlp_posthoc`).

## A3. The Fragility Index, exactly as implemented

From `sal/fi.py` (`extract_activation_graph`, `compute_fi`). Every step below is in the shipped code, not a description of intent.

**1. What the nodes are.** One node per attention head: `n_layers x n_heads` nodes, ordered layer-major. On ViT-B/16 that is 12 x 12 = 144 nodes; on BERT-base 144; on DeiT-small 72; on DINOv2-large 384; on Phi-2 1024.

**2. What is captured.** A forward **pre-hook** on each attention output projection records that projection's INPUT, i.e. the concatenated per-head attention outputs. This is the only tensor whose feature dimensions map one-to-one onto heads; the projection's output is already mixed across heads.

**3. Per-head signature.** For each captured batch the sequence dimension is reduced to one vector per sentence: a **mask-weighted mean** `(x * attention_mask).sum(1) / mask.sum(1)` where an attention mask exists, and a plain mean over the sequence where it does not (vision models). The result is reshaped to `[sentences, heads, head_dim]`; head *h*'s signature is `[:, h, :]` **flattened over sentences and head_dim**, cast to float64. With `num_samples` sentences and head_dim *d*, each signature is a vector of length `num_samples * d`.

**4. Similarity.** Row-wise **Pearson correlation** between signatures: mean-centre each row, L2-normalise (with a 1e-12 guard), take the Gram matrix. The result is signed and symmetric.

**5. Edges.** The graph is **undirected and unweighted**. The threshold is not fixed in similarity units but chosen **per graph to hit a target edge density**: with `density = 0.10` (the default), the threshold is the 90th percentile of `|S|` over the strict upper triangle, and an edge exists where `|S| > threshold`. The diagonal is zeroed. Taking the ABSOLUTE value means strong anti-correlation is an edge too.

**6. The index.** With `A` the binary adjacency and `A2 = A @ A`, an edge `(i, j)` has **zero triangle support** when `A2[i, j] == 0`, i.e. heads *i* and *j* share no common neighbour. FI is the fraction of edges with zero triangle support, over the strict upper triangle. Range [0, 1]; 0 when every edge sits in at least one triangle, and **1 by convention when the graph has no edges at all**.

**7. What follows from the definition.** FI is a *fraction of the edges of this graph*. Because the threshold targets a fixed density, the edge COUNT is approximately `density * n(n-1)/2` and therefore grows quadratically with the number of heads: 1030 edges at 144 nodes, 256 at 72, 52 378 at 1024. One edge is worth 1/1030 in the first case and 1/52378 in the last, so **an FI difference has no fixed meaning across graphs of different sizes** -- including the same model before and after head pruning. This is a property of the definition, not a measurement artefact, and it is why the paper reports FI only within a fixed graph.

**8. Layer classification.** `classify_layers` removes each layer's heads and reads the RELATIVE change in FI; below 1% the layer is IMMUNE, at or above the critical threshold (shipped default 0.25) it is CRITICAL, otherwise BUFFER. This is a product API layered on the index above, not part of it.

### A4.2 Calibration activations: ours are dense

A deliberate, disclosed difference from the reference implementations of SparseGPT and Wanda, which it is the reader's business to know before comparing a number of ours with a number of theirs.

Both references walk the decoder **sequentially**: a layer's Hessian (SparseGPT) or activation norm (Wanda) is accumulated from a model whose **earlier layers are already pruned**, so each layer is fitted to the input it will actually receive at inference. Ours accumulates every target's statistics from the **dense** model in one calibration pass, and every figure in this work was measured that way.

The expectation is that our one-shot numbers should be, if anything, slightly *worse* than a sequential implementation's at the same sparsity, because a sequential pass can compensate for upstream error. The measurement neither confirms nor refutes that, and the table below is generated from `results/opt67_gate/gate_verdict.json` rather than written out, because the first draft of this paragraph got the sign wrong and drew a conclusion from it.

| cell | measured | published | difference | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dense` | 10.8601 | 10.8600 | **+0.0001** | no calibration |
| `sparsegpt50` | 11.6766 | 11.5500 | **+0.1266** | worse |
| `sparsegpt2to4` | 14.0128 | 14.2000 | **-0.1872** | better |
| `magnitude50` | 968.9918 | 969.0000 | **-0.0082** | no calibration |

Across all four cells the agreement is within **0.19 perplexity** in absolute value. Only 2 of them involve calibration data at all: the dense row is a pure evaluation and magnitude pruning reads only the weights, so neither says anything about this choice. Of the 2 calibration-sensitive cells, 1 lands above the published value and 1 below. **There is no systematic direction**, and on a sample of 2 there could not be one: these numbers show that the grouped dense-activation calibration reproduces the published values to within +/-0.2 perplexity, and they do **not** establish that the choice is neutral, nor that it is unfavourable to us.


Memory forced a second implementation detail that is **not** a semantic one. Holding one pruner per target needs 42.0 GiB of fp32 Hessians on OPT-6.7B (7.9 at 1.3B), which with fp32 weights exceeds a 64 GiB MI250X GCD, so the 192 target layers are pruned in **8 groups of 24** with one calibration pass each. Each group's pruned weights are buffered and the originals restored before the next pass, so **every pass still sees the dense model**; `sal-torch/tests/test_sparse_grouping.py` requires grouped and whole-model passes to produce bit-identical masks and weights for both pruners, at four group sizes, unstructured and 2:4. Grouping therefore changes the memory and the runtime, not the measurement.

A third memory detail, of the same kind. Mask-preserving recovery has to re-apply the sparsity mask after every optimiser step, so a mask is resident for the whole fine-tune, and at 6.7B a mask is a large object. It is held as `bool`, one byte an entry. It was previously cast to the weight's own dtype, which on an fp32 model spends four bytes to record a single zero: 6.00 GiB of mask a rank where 1.50 GiB says the same thing. The sharded path also built a second, full-size copy before sharding which it then never read, because the mask has to be re-read from the parameters once they are sharded; that copy is no longer built at all. **Neither change moves a reported number**: multiplying a float by `False` is exactly zero and by `True` exactly itself, in any float dtype, with no rounding either way, and the dropped copy was dead weight. What they changed is whether the job finishes. Three identical attempts at the 6.7B SparseGPT 50% cell died in the first backward pass of recovery on a 64 GiB GCD, one of them 770 MiB short with 424 MiB free; with the first change the same arm crossed that point and ran to the end, with 1.66 GiB of the GCD's reserved capacity to spare. Recovery at this scale is memory-bound at the margin, and that is a property of the recipe worth stating beside the results it produced.

### A4.3 WikiText-2 perplexity, exactly as computed

| step | what the code does |
|---|---|
| split | `wikitext-2-raw-v1`, test |
| joining | all documents joined with `"\n\n"` into ONE string, then tokenised in a single pass with the model's own tokenizer |
| windowing | `n = total_tokens // 2048` **non-overlapping** windows, full stride |
| final window | **dropped**: the trailing `total_tokens % 2048` tokens are not scored |
| per window | `model(window, labels=window)`; HF's causal loss averages over the 2047 predicted positions |
| accumulation | `total_nll += loss * 2047`, `total_tok += 2047` |
| perplexity | `exp(total_nll / total_tok)`, a token-weighted mean NLL |
| precision | **float32, no autocast**, unlike the ViT scoring |

This is the SparseGPT/Wanda protocol, which is what makes the dense gate meaningful: we read 14.6240 against the 14.62 those papers publish.

### A4.5 Compute units

**Three** quantities appear in this project, not two, and only the middle one is the campaign's size. Every compute figure states which it is.

| quantity | what it counts | the campaign total |
|---|---|---|
| elapsed h | wall-clock time of the jobs, whatever they held | 2046.0 |
| **GCD-h** | allocated GCDs x elapsed, from sacct's `AllocTRES` | **2782.2** |
| billed GPU-h | the allocation's unit: one GPU is one MI250X module of two GCDs, so GCD-h / 2 | **1391.1** |

The first two rows differ because `standard-g` is **exclusive**: wave 5's OPT-6.7B jobs were allocated -- and billed -- all 8 GCDs of a node for their whole wall time, using 4 of them for FSDP. A sum of elapsed hours therefore under-reports the campaign by 736.2 GCD-h. Earlier revisions of this file made exactly that substitution.

All three figures are this campaign's own, summed over the jobs whose logs are in its log directory and joined to `sacct` by job id. ### A4.7 The logged learning rate is one step ahead

Every training loop in the campaign steps the scheduler immediately after the optimizer, and the two decoder recovery loops then log the rate, so **each learning rate they log is the one the next step will use** and a run's last log line carries a rate no update ever consumed. The vision loops log no learning rate at all. The order in each shipped loop, read from its source:

| loop | order |
|---|---|
| `run_sal_qat_prototype.py:train_variant` | `scheduler.step()` (this loop logs no learning rate) |
| `run_sal_qat_prototype.py:make_step` | `optimizer.step()` (this loop logs no learning rate) |
| `exp23_baselines.py:recover` | `scheduler.step()` (this loop logs no learning rate) |
| `exp36_opt_sparse.py:recover` | `opt.step()` then `sched.step()` then `get_last_lr()` |
| `exp36_fsdp.py:recover_fsdp` | `opt.step()` then `sched.step()` then `get_last_lr()` |

Both schedulers are therefore stepped as many times as there are optimizer steps, so `last_epoch` reaches `total_steps`. What sits there, computed by stepping the schedules:

| recipe | rate the final update used | rate after the final scheduler step |
|---|---|---|
| vision: LambdaLR, linear warmup 5% then cosine, 40038 steps | 8.527e-14 | **0** |
| decoder: OneCycleLR, max_lr 1e-05, pct_start 0.10, 3906 steps | 4e-11 | **4.2e-11** |

The vision value is exactly zero because that schedule clamps its cosine argument at one; the decoder value sits a little above its own floor because `OneCycleLR` does not clamp the within-phase percentage, and one step past the end the cosine turns back upward.

**No published figure is affected.** No optimizer step consumes either value, and no result JSON records a learning rate read from a scheduler: the only reader is the per-step log line. The off-by-one rates therefore exist in the job logs, which are in the archive, and nowhere else. The product's own recovery stage does not take that final step at all.

### A6.1 FFN against heads on three denominators

Both arms remove about the same number of parameters; they do not remove the same compute, and they do not buy the same speed. **Cheapness is the accuracy cost per unit of resource freed**, so each denominator gives its own factor.

Accuracy cost against the 81.554% 2-epoch control: heads 50% costs **3.912pp**, FFN 25% costs **1.251pp** (random neuron selection, the like-for-like arm).

| denominator | heads 50% | FFN 25% | cost per unit, heads | cost per unit, FFN | FFN cheaper by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| parameters removed | 16.37% | 16.36% | 0.2390 | 0.0765 | **3.13x** |
| FLOPs removed | 17.91% | 15.88% | 0.2184 | 0.0788 | **2.77x** |
| latency saved at batch 32 (1 - 1/speedup) | 16.90% | 12.85% | 0.2315 | 0.0974 | **2.38x** |

**The FFN is cheaper on every denominator, and the factor shrinks as the denominator moves from parameters toward what a deployment actually buys**: 3.13x per parameter, 2.77x per FLOP, 2.38x per point of latency saved. The direction is the robust part; the magnitude depends on what is being counted, which is why the paper states all three rather than one.

Note what the middle and right columns of the table above also say, and what an earlier draft of this section got backwards: at the matched parameter budget heads remove MORE compute (17.91% of FLOPs against 15.88%) and buy MORE speed (1.203x against 1.147x). Removing more is not the same as being cheaper to remove, and the two readings point opposite ways. Only the cost-per-unit columns answer "which should I prune first".
### A6.2 Why batch 1 shows no speedup

Attention is 35.8% of a dense ViT-B/16 block's FLOPs, so removing half the heads removes 17.9% of the model's compute and removing half the FFN removes 31.8%. At batch 32 the measured speedups (1.203x and 1.345x) track those fractions closely. At batch 1 every arm reads about 1.00x, which no FLOP count explains: the model is not compute-bound there. **A6 cannot settle that; the `rocprof` profile in B4 is the measurement that can.**

## A8. The decoder same-seed rerun floor, measured

The vision bands could be widened by a floor that had been observed, 0.040-0.170pp over four dense-control pairs. The decoder had none, so the v0.7.0 gate's G3 bands were built on a **declared** 0.10 perplexity, and G3-paper then failed on it. These pairs measure what was declared. Each one is exp36's own unchanged command line, re-run against the archive job that first produced the value, with no `PYTHONPATH` front-run so the re-run takes the sal the archive ran.
